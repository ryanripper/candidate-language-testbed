# Extending the Candidate Twitter-Language Research — Extension Plan & 2025–26 Literature Scan

*Prepared July 2026. Extends Ryan's prior research comparing congressional candidates through the language of their tweets and retweets, using the methods compiled in [cutting-edge-data-science-skills-2026.md](../skills/cutting-edge-data-science-skills-2026.md). Focus areas per Ryan: embeddings & retrieval, LLM-augmented topic modeling & classification, and evaluation & rigor. Revised 2026-07-20: §4 and Phase 2 updated per Ryan's NOTES.md topic-modeling entry — classical baselines (LDA/NMF/LSA) added and a blind topic-recovery study on the synthetic testbed made the Phase 2 opener.*

---

## 1. The original research, restated as a measurement problem

The prior work: collect tweets and retweets for all candidates running for Congress, treat both as language the candidate "said" (a retweet signaling agreement with the original), embed the language (pretrained word2vec vs. word2vec trained on the collected tweets vs. AI-trained embeddings), reduce dimensionality with PCA, and compare candidates via distance measures in the resulting space. Three pain points defined the practical ceiling, and all three sit on the *data-collection* side: scraping tweets for all candidates took many hours per full run, that scrape had to be re-run frequently to capture updated content, and assembling the candidate roster itself was tedious — with Twitter's API pricing ultimately making continued collection effectively impossible. The methods below matter, but §8 (data strategy) is where the project's binding constraint actually gets solved: the 2026 answer is to stop scraping per-account entirely, in favor of push-based streams and bulk downloadable corpora.

It helps to name what this research *is* in the current literature's terms, because that unlocks a whole body of directly relevant work. A 2025 review in *Quality & Quantity* ([Computational Measurement of Political Positions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13238)) organizes text-based political position estimation into four families: word-frequency methods (Wordscores/Wordfish), topic-model-based methods, **word-embedding-based methods (your approach — "Party Embeddings", SemScale)**, and the newest, LLM-prompt-based methods. Your project is a Type III method in their typology, and their central recommendations — balance complexity against transparency, and above all *validate against gold standards* — map directly onto the extension phases below. The exciting part is that the field has now produced Type IV (LLM) methods that can serve as an independent second instrument for validating your embedding-space distances, rather than replacing them.

Each subsection below takes one design decision from the original pipeline and pairs it with what 2025–26 methods offer.

## 2. Extension 1 — The embedding layer: from word2vec to contextual, sentence-level, and *fast static* models

**What changes.** Word2vec assigns one vector per word regardless of context, so "border" in an immigration tweet and "border" in a trade tweet collapse to the same point — exactly the kind of distinction that separates candidates. The upgrade path has three tiers:

1. **Sentence-transformer embeddings per tweet.** Embed each tweet (and retweeted text) with a modern sentence-transformer rather than averaging word vectors. Candidate representations become distributions of tweet vectors instead of a single averaged point — enabling distance measures between *distributions* (energy distance, MMD, Wasserstein) rather than centroid-to-centroid distance, which is strictly more informative. A directly analogous study, [Analyzing political party positions through multi-language twitter text embeddings](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11169868/), did party-level positioning from tweet embeddings and is a useful template and baseline.
2. **Stability-aware contextual embeddings.** A concern unique to political science: contextual embeddings can be *unstable* (retrain, get different geometry — bad for a measurement instrument). [Achieving Semantic Consistency: Contextualized Word Representations for Political Text Analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04505v2) addresses exactly this tension between word2vec's stability and transformers' context-sensitivity; worth reading before committing to an embedding model, and it argues for the kind of pretrained-vs-corpus-trained comparison you already ran — now at the contextual level.
3. **Modern embedding compute is cheap — don't let it become the new bottleneck.** The original bottleneck was scraping, not embedding, and upgrading to transformers shouldn't introduce a compute problem where there wasn't one. [Model2Vec](https://github.com/MinishLab/model2vec) distills a sentence transformer into a static embedding model that is [~50x smaller and up to ~500x faster](https://huggingface.co/blog/Pringled/model2vec), running on CPU; Hugging Face's [static-embeddings training recipe](https://huggingface.co/blog/static-embeddings) reports ~100–400x CPU speedups at 85%+ of quality. Embedding every tweet from every congressional candidate stays a minutes-scale job on a laptop even after the contextual upgrade. A sensible design: Model2Vec for the full-corpus sweep, full sentence transformer (or a late-interaction/ColBERT-style model, skills doc §1.3) for the subsets where precision matters, and [matryoshka embeddings](../skills/cutting-edge-data-science-skills-2026.md) to tune the cost/quality dial.

**Why it matters.** The three-way comparison you originally ran (pretrained vs. corpus-trained vs. AI-trained) becomes far more interesting at this tier: pretrained static (Model2Vec), corpus-adapted sentence transformer, and API-based LLM embeddings, with agreement between them as a robustness check rather than just a horse race.

## 3. Extension 2 — The geometry: beyond PCA, and being honest about distances

PCA remains defensible for *measurement* (linear, deterministic, interpretable axes — often axis 1 correlates with partisanship), but three refinements from the recent literature apply. First, for *cluster structure and visualization*, the field has moved to UMAP (often feeding HDBSCAN), which preserves local neighborhood structure PCA smears out — see this [comparative study of UMAP and other dimensionality-reduction methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02275v1) and the practical comparison from the [GDELT project on PCA vs UMAP for clustering news embeddings](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/visualizing-an-entire-day-of-global-news-coverage-technical-experiments-pca-vs-umap-for-hdbscan-t-sne-dimensionality-reduction/). The two roles should be kept separate: PCA (or no reduction at all) for distances you report, UMAP only for pictures — nonlinear maps distort global distances by construction. Second, transformer embedding spaces are *anisotropic* (vectors bunch in a narrow cone), so raw cosine distances compress; standard fixes are mean-centering and whitening before computing distances. Third, and most consequential: a candidate-to-candidate distance conflates **what they talk about** (topic mix) with **how they talk about it** (stance/framing). Extension 3 is the fix.

## 4. Extension 3 — LLM-augmented topic modeling and topic-conditioned comparison

This is the highest-value analytical upgrade. Pipeline: embed tweets → cluster with **BERTopic** → have an LLM label and describe each topic (skills doc §1.6) → recompute candidate distances *within* each topic. Two candidates far apart overall might be nearly identical on veterans' issues and maximally distant on immigration — that decomposition is a publishable finding in itself, and it converts the original "who is far from whom" result into "far from whom, *on what*."

The template exists: [Modeling Political Discourse with Sentence-BERT and BERTopic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22904) (Oct 2025) does exactly this on political text, and a prior study applied [BERTopic to the 117th Congress's tweets](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378337159_Topic_Extraction_BERTopic's_Insight_into_the_117th_Congress's_Twitterverse), giving you a baseline taxonomy of congressional Twitter topics to compare against. BERTopic's [dynamic topic modeling](https://maartengr.github.io/BERTopic/getting_started/topicsovertime/topicsovertime.html) adds the time axis — how candidate attention shifts across a campaign — which your repeated-collection design was already implicitly after. Seeded/guided topic modeling (skills doc §1.6) lets you pin the taxonomy to a fixed issue list (e.g., CampaignView's policy areas, below) so results are comparable across cycles.

**Keep the classical topic models in the race** *(added 2026-07-20 from NOTES.md)*. BERTopic should not enter unopposed. The embeddings/PCA analysis on the synthetic testbed taught exactly this lesson — the "dumb" TF-IDF+SVD baseline beat corpus-trained word2vec — and the classical topic-model family (LDA, NMF, LSA) plays the same baseline role here. Note that LSA *is* SVD on the (TF-IDF-weighted) document-term matrix — i.e., the method that already hit r = .974 on the testbed's ideology axis; LDA and NMF are its probabilistic and parts-based siblings, and comparative studies (e.g., [a 2025 coherence/perplexity evaluation of LDA, NMF, and BERTopic](https://www.iieta.org/journals/isi/paper/10.18280/isi.301208), [a news-headline comparison of the same trio](https://publikasi.dinus.ac.id/index.php/jcta/article/view/11635)) find the classical methods remain competitive on short, focused corpora — precisely what tweets are. Better still, the synthetic corpus carries a planted `true_topic` column that the 2026-07-20 analysis sealed and never used. That turns the "Is AI good enough to identify themes?" question from NOTES.md into a runnable experiment: a **blind topic-recovery bake-off** — LDA vs. NMF vs. BERTopic vs. direct LLM theming, each scored against the planted topics (adjusted Rand / NMI on tweet-topic assignments) plus interpretability metrics (topic coherence, and an LLM-as-judge or human read of label quality per skills doc §1.7). That design also addresses the NOTES.md worry that discovered topics come out too broad, too inclusive, or uninterpretable: coherence scores and recovery-vs-ground-truth quantify that fear instead of leaving it an impression, and seeded topic modeling is the mitigation if it materializes.

On top of topics, **stance**: zero-shot and instruction-tuned LLM classification (skills doc §1.5) can score each tweet's position on the issue it discusses, turning topic-conditioned distances into interpretable *positional* differences. Recent work shows both the promise — [Törnberg 2025: LLMs outperform expert coders and supervised classifiers at annotating political social media messages](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/08944393241286471), [Stay Tuned: improving sentiment analysis and stance detection with LLMs (Political Analysis)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/stay-tuned-improving-sentiment-analysis-and-stance-detection-using-large-language-models/2D8F121012D3D1CB2259B6DD5EE32D0D), and [target-stance extraction for unpacking complex political opinions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23531) — and the pitfalls: [LLMs shift their labels when party cues are visible](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05834-4), and a [PLOS One study documents systematic failure modes in LLM actor-stance identification](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0335547). Practical rule from this literature: strip party/author identifiers before LLM annotation, and calibrate on a human-labeled subset.

## 5. Extension 4 — A second, independent instrument: LLM positioning

The most striking Type IV development: [Positioning Political Texts with Large Language Models by Asking and Averaging](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/positioning-political-texts-with-large-language-models-by-asking-and-averaging/BB17F58E7329B53BC4B5F4065C7E00FF) (*Political Analysis*) simply asks an LLM to place a text on an ideological scale, repeats, and averages — achieving correlations with expert benchmarks around .90 on party manifestos and campaign text. Related: [Benoit et al. in the AJPS on LLMs analyzing political texts via natural language understanding](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ajps.70050), and evidence that [linear representations of political perspective emerge inside LLMs](https://openreview.net/forum?id=rwqShzb9li) (ICLR) — meaning LLM internal activations themselves encode a left–right axis you can probe. Running ask-and-average over each candidate's tweets gives a per-candidate ideological score from a *completely different method family* than your embedding distances. Convergence between the two is a strong validity argument; divergence identifies exactly the candidates worth a case study. Note the review's caveat about LLM opacity and training-data contamination ([arXiv 2511.13238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13238)) — which is precisely why the design should treat LLM scores as a *second instrument*, not ground truth.

## 6. Extension 5 — Taking the retweet assumption seriously

Treating retweets as endorsed speech was a substantive modeling choice, and the literature broadly backs it — a [meta-analysis of retweet meaning](https://cs.wellesley.edu/~pmetaxas/WorkingPapers/Retweet-meaning.pdf) finds retweets predominantly signal agreement, endorsement, and trust — but the extension is to convert the assumption into a measured quantity. [Retweet-BERT](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/download/22160/21939/26223) (ICWSM) estimates political leaning by *combining* language content with the retweet network, and the older [Quantifying Political Leaning from Tweets, Retweets, and Retweeters](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301571965_Quantifying_Political_Leaning_from_Tweets_Retweets_and_Retweeters) line models tweets and retweets as separate but linked signals. Concretely: compute each candidate's position twice (original tweets only vs. retweets only), report the divergence, and use a retweet-weight sensitivity analysis (λ·retweets + (1−λ)·tweets, sweep λ) so reviewers can see the conclusion isn't an artifact of the endorsement assumption. Where quote-tweets are distinguishable, they deserve their own channel — the same meta-analysis notes commentary can invert meaning.

## 7. Extension 6 — Evaluation & rigor: the harness that makes it publishable

Per the skills doc's own ordering (§1.7 first), build the eval layer before the analyses:

- **Golden dataset.** A few hundred human-labeled tweets (topic + stance) as the fixed regression suite for every LLM-assisted component; judge calibration, pairwise comparisons, and bias controls per the LLM-as-judge best practices in the skills doc.
- **External validity anchors.** For incumbents and repeat candidates, correlate embedding-space positions with DW-NOMINATE roll-call scores and expert placements — the standard validation move in the ideal-point literature ([review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13238)).
- **Instrument robustness.** Report agreement (e.g., Procrustes-aligned correlations of distance matrices) across embedding choices, with/without retweets, PCA vs. no reduction, and embedding-distance vs. LLM ask-and-average. Stability across instruments *is* the finding that elevates this from a demo to a measurement paper.
- **Method invariance check.** Run the pipeline on a fixed historical corpus (e.g., archived 117th Congress tweets) where facts are settled, before applying it to new data.

## 8. Data strategy — replacing the Twitter API and the scraping model itself

This is where the project's binding constraint gets solved. The hours-long collection runs were a consequence of the *pull* model: visit every candidate's account, page through their timeline, repeat frequently for updates, and fight rate limits the whole way. Every option below eliminates per-account scraping rather than just making it cheaper:

- **Bluesky** is the strongest live replacement, and it inverts the collection model: the AT Protocol **firehose** (or the lighter Jetstream service) is a single push-based stream of *all* public posts — you subscribe once, filter server-side or locally for your candidate handles, and updates arrive continuously instead of being re-scraped. Collection stops being a batch job that takes hours and becomes a small always-on listener. There is no API paywall, and political-science-grade resources already exist — [PolitiSky24](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07606), a 2024-election stance dataset (16k user-target stance pairs, ~2.8M posts, LLM-labeled pipeline at 81% validated accuracy, [openly available on Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15616911)); a [year-scale Bluesky corpus](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0310330); and studies of [Bluesky's political network structure](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0318034). Caveat honestly: its user base skews left ([Pew, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/05/29/bluesky-has-caught-on-with-many-news-influencers-but-x-remains-popular/)), so it measures candidate *language*, not a representative electorate.
- **CampaignView** ([Scientific Data, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-05491-x)) — an open database of policy platforms and biographical narratives for congressional candidates, which directly solves the "assembling all candidates was tiresome" problem: it's the roster *and* a per-candidate policy text corpus in one, downloadable in bulk with zero scraping.
- **Congressional press releases and archives** — [Congress Press](https://congressionaldata.org/congress-press/) ([GitHub](https://github.com/dwillis/congress-press)) for ongoing official communication, and the [congresstweets archive](https://github.com/alexlitel/congresstweets) for historical Twitter output of Congress. Both are bulk `git clone`/download corpora — the hours-of-scraping step simply doesn't exist for them — which is what makes them ideal as the frozen methods-development corpus in §7.
- **Multi-platform reality.** Candidates now split across X, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and Bluesky — [Candidates Be Posting (2025)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/20563051251337541) documents multi-platform strategy in the 2022 midterms and is the design reference for treating *platform* as a variable rather than an inconvenience.

## 9. Phased roadmap

**Phase 0 — Corpus & harness (the foundation).** Assemble the frozen historical corpus (congresstweets + CampaignView + press releases); build the golden dataset and eval harness (§7). No findings yet, but everything after this is measurable.

**Phase 1 — Embedding upgrade & replication.** Re-run the original design (candidate centroids, PCA, distances) on the frozen corpus with the three-tier embedding comparison (§2), including Model2Vec for the full sweep. Deliverable: a replication + method-comparison result — does the word2vec-era geometry survive modern embeddings?

**Phase 2 — Topic-conditioned analysis.** First, on the synthetic testbed: the blind topic-recovery bake-off (LDA vs. NMF vs. BERTopic vs. LLM theming, scored against planted `true_topic` with coherence/interpretability metrics — §4), reusing the seal-then-validate protocol from the 2026-07-20 embeddings analysis. Then, on the frozen real corpus: the winning method + LLM labeling; within-topic distances; dynamic topics over the campaign timeline (§4). Deliverables: an empirical answer to "does classical topic modeling still earn its keep next to AI?" and the "far from whom, on what" decomposition.

**Phase 3 — Stance & the second instrument.** LLM stance scoring per topic and ask-and-average ideological positioning (§4–5); retweet sensitivity analysis (§6); validation against DW-NOMINATE (§7). Deliverable: the measurement-validity paper this project wants to be.

**Phase 4 — Live pipeline.** Port collection to the Bluesky firehose/Jetstream listener + press-release feeds; refresh becomes continuous and free rather than an hours-long re-scrape; dashboard of candidate positions over time.

Phases 0–2 involve no scraping at all (bulk-download corpora only) and no paid APIs — the constraints that ended the original project are gone at exactly the tier where the most interesting extensions live.
